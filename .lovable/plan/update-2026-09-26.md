# Rebuild the Liel Wainberg Portfolio

## 01 — Experience & Creative Direction

- Rebuild the homepage as one continuous, authored experience rather than a collection of conventional portfolio sections.

- The narrative should move through: Arrival → Liel → Digital → Selected Work → Canine → Hospitality / LW.LIQUID → Return to Liel → Contact.

- The website should feel like entering Liel Wainberg's digital world. Liel is the central identity; the three worlds are expressions of the same person, not separate brands.

- Create a fast, premium, logo-led opening experience using the real Liel Wainberg identity assets already present in the project. It should feel intentional and cinematic without delaying access to the site.

- Build a sophisticated Hero that immediately establishes who Liel is and what he builds, using the real portrait/assets from the project. Do not replace real assets with AI-generated people, stock photography, generic 3D characters or avatars.

- Create a clear, elegant navigation system. It should feel like a premium portfolio navigation, not a futuristic control panel.

- Use natural scrolling with scroll-triggered choreography, sticky scenes where appropriate, progressive reveals, controlled parallax, environmental transitions and layered composition.

- Never hijack scrolling. The user must always feel in control.

- Make transitions between Digital, Canine and Hospitality feel like genuine changes of environment rather than color changes between sections.

- Digital should feel precise, architectural, technical, dark and intelligent.

- Canine should feel grounded, natural, tactile, calm and serious, using Burgundy within a restrained natural palette.

- Hospitality / LW.LIQUID should feel warm, atmospheric, social and crafted, using its own contextual Copper / Amber / Teal / Turquoise accent.

- The visual language must evolve between worlds while retaining a recognizable Liel Wainberg identity.

- End by returning from the three worlds to the person behind them. The final contact experience should feel human and direct rather than like a sales funnel.

- Treat the entire homepage like a film: arrival, recognition, curiosity, depth, discovery, environmental change, discovery again, return and contact.

- Create visual rhythm by alternating typography, photography, real project imagery, information density, negative space, interaction and quiet moments.

- Avoid repetitive section templates. Every major section should have a reason to exist and a distinct visual role.

- Resolve conflicting guidance into one coherent design language. Do not combine unrelated design systems or create a visually inconsistent collection of patterns.

- Do not blindly copy references from any Skill. Use the principles and knowledge to create an original Liel Wainberg experience.

## 03 — Content & Humanization

- Preserve all verified existing projects, case studies, factual personal information, real imagery, testimonials, contact information and meaningful existing content.

- Inspect the existing project before rewriting anything.

- Do not invent personal history, clients, projects, technologies, metrics, awards, testimonials, results, years of experience or achievements.

- Rewrite public-facing Hebrew and English copy wherever necessary so it sounds like a real person wrote it.

- The copy should be direct, intelligent, specific, calm, confident and human.

- Remove generic agency language, corporate language, exaggerated claims, unnecessary philosophical statements and repetitive marketing formulas.

- Remove unnecessary em dashes, decorative punctuation patterns, excessive semicolons and artificial three-part adjective structures.

- Avoid obvious AI-generated portfolio language such as "building the future", "cutting-edge", "seamless experiences", "unlocking potential", "transforming ideas", "pushing boundaries", "redefining", "at the intersection of", unless a specific factual context genuinely requires such wording.

- Do not simply replace AI vocabulary with synonyms. Rewrite the underlying sentences naturally.

- Do not make every sentence sound like a slogan.

- Do not over-polish the writing until it loses personality.

- Hebrew and English must each feel naturally written in their own language rather than translated versions of one another.

- Preserve strong existing copy when it already feels authentic.

- Remove placeholders, fake proof, unsupported claims and redundant system terminology.

## 04 — Professional Positioning

- Establish LIEL WAINBERG as the primary brand and identity.

- Do not position the site as TarraCoata, an agency, an AI developer, an AI-first developer, a software company or a generic web designer.

- Communicate that Liel builds software, systems, applications, digital products, SaaS, web applications, e-commerce systems, APIs, integrations, automation and UX/UI-driven digital experiences.

- Websites are one capability, not the ceiling of the positioning.

- AI should remain a tool rather than become Liel's public identity.

- Preserve LW.SYS as a subtle visual language for metadata, coordinates, system labels and technical details, but keep it secondary.

- Maintain an approximate Human 70 / System 30 balance.

- The visitor should understand both what Liel builds and who Liel is.

## 05 — Selected Work & Case Studies

- Make Selected Work one of the strongest parts of the experience.

- Replace generic card-grid presentation with an editorial portfolio system.

- Use large real project imagery, interface details, contextual information, subtle metadata and concise project narratives.

- Every major project should communicate what it is, what Liel's role was, what was designed/built, relevant technical depth and the verified outcome where available.

- Structure important case studies around Problem → Approach → Design → Architecture → Build → Result, but keep the presentation concise and visually sophisticated.

- Preserve existing project routes and verified project information.

- Do not invent technical stacks simply to make projects appear more impressive.

- Present technical depth progressively so technical visitors can explore it without overwhelming non-technical visitors.

- Favor real work and evidence over decorative filler.

## 06 — Design System & Visual Language

- Consolidate the implementation into one coherent semantic design system.

- Establish tokens for:

  - Background

  - Surfaces

  - Text

  - Muted text

  - Borders

  - Digital accent

  - Canine accent

  - Hospitality accent

  - Typography

  - Spacing

  - Radius

  - Shadows

  - Motion

  - Responsive breakpoints

- Global foundation:

  - Deep Black: #050505

  - Surface: #0B0B0B

  - Text: #FAFAFA

  - Digital Purple: #6A00FF

  - Soft Purple: #8B5CF6

  - Light Purple: #C4B5FD

- Purple must remain restrained and contextual rather than becoming the entire visual identity.

- Canine should introduce Burgundy through a natural neutral environment.

- Hospitality should use its own warm contextual accent rather than inheriting Digital Purple.

- Typography should combine sophisticated editorial display typography, highly readable body typography and restrained technical mono typography.

- Treat typography as a primary compositional tool.

- Every button, link, navigation state, card, project metadata element, image treatment, separator, border and interaction state should feel intentionally designed.

- Avoid random gradients, excessive glassmorphism, generic blobs, meaningless 3D objects, decorative code and visual filler.

## 07 — Motion, Interaction & Transitions

- Motion should feel expensive, restrained and purposeful.

- Use animation for hierarchy, storytelling, environmental transition, depth, discovery and interaction feedback.

- Avoid animation everywhere.

- Avoid excessive particles, random floating elements, aggressive parallax and flashy transitions.

- Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

- Use scroll progression to create a feeling of movement through environments without breaking native scrolling.

- Ensure transitions remain performant on mobile devices.

- Every animation must have a clear UX or narrative purpose.

## 08 — Hebrew, English, RTL/LTR & Mobile

- Treat Hebrew and English as first-class experiences.

- Correctly handle lang, dir, alignment, line length, line height, punctuation, spacing, navigation and animation direction.

- Hebrew must feel designed for Hebrew rather than English translated into RTL.

- Mobile must be designed independently rather than treated as a compressed desktop.

- Optimize specifically for iPhone and small touch screens.

- Check safe areas, typography, touch targets, fixed controls, navigation, scrolling, imagery, transitions and section composition.

- No horizontal overflow.

- No floating controls covering important content.

- No dead space created by desktop layouts collapsing poorly on mobile.

## 09 — Accessibility

- Meet strong WCAG AA fundamentals throughout the experience.

- Use semantic HTML and logical heading hierarchy.

- Provide visible focus states.

- Ensure keyboard navigation works.

- Ensure contrast is sufficient.

- Use accessible buttons, links and forms.

- Maintain appropriate touch target sizes.

- Provide meaningful image alt text where appropriate.

- Mark decorative imagery as decorative.

- Support screen readers.

- Respect prefers-reduced-motion.

- Keep accessibility and language controls functional, responsive and visually integrated.

- Never allow accessibility controls to cover navigation, CTAs, imagery or meaningful content.

## 10 — SEO / GEO / AEO

- Treat search visibility and machine readability as part of the architecture, not an afterthought.

- Establish Liel Wainberg as the primary entity of the website.

- Implement accurate Person and ProfilePage structured data where appropriate.

- Create a stable @id for the primary Liel Wainberg entity.

- Use accurate sameAs relationships only for genuine online identities.

- Clearly establish the relationship between Liel and his work, capabilities, projects, Canine world and Hospitality world.

- Implement accurate project structured data only where supported by visible factual content.

- Use semantic HTML with a logical H1 → H2 → H3 hierarchy.

- Ensure important information exists as crawlable text and is never dependent solely on WebGL, canvas, hover states or animation.

- Make factual answers easy to extract naturally:

  - Who is Liel Wainberg?

  - What does Liel Wainberg do?

  - What does Liel build?

  - What technologies and disciplines does he work with?

  - What projects has he built?

  - What is CANINE.BEHAVIOR?

  - What is LW.LIQUID?

- Use answer-first writing for factual concepts without creating artificial FAQ spam.

- Make project information atomic and explicit: project, type, role, approach, technology, result where verified.

- Make authorship clear throughout the site.

- Use first-party information and real project experience as the primary source of authority.

- Implement unique, meaningful metadata for every indexable route.

- Implement accurate canonical URLs.

- Implement sitemap.xml and robots.txt correctly.

- Implement hreflang only if the actual routing architecture supports separate indexable language URLs. Do not invent locale URLs.

- Implement high-quality Open Graph and social metadata.

- Optimize important image filenames, alt text, dimensions, formats and surrounding semantic context.

- Ensure internal links use descriptive anchor text.

- Ensure AI retrieval systems can understand the identity, work, authorship and relationships without reconstructing meaning from decorative UI.

- Do not use hidden text, keyword stuffing, fake FAQs, fake reviews, fake entities, schema spam, doorway pages, duplicate SEO pages or AI-generated filler.

- Validate structured data and inspect the final rendered document for crawlability and semantic correctness.

- Treat GEO as citation and retrieval readiness, not as an algorithmic manipulation technique.

- The final website should be useful to both humans and search/answer systems.
